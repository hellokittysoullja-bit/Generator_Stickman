# Why voiceover "explainer" Reels underperform vs relatable / meme / POV / skit / series formats, and what drives sends (DM shares)

Scope: evidence on (1) the psychology of sharing, (2) platform statements on what Instagram ranks, (3) length / format data, (4) why small accounts' Reels stall, including off-platform CTAs. The aim is to judge a small account whose 30–45 s drawn-cat voiceover explainers (captions, cognitive-dissonance hook, CTA to Telegram/profile) get 100–200 views.

Evidence-strength legend: **[A]** peer-reviewed study or first-party platform statement; **[B]** large vendor dataset with stated method; **[C]** vendor/creator blog, small sample, or secondary report; **[D]** anecdote or unsourced.

Research limits: about 18 tool calls. Several full texts (Berger 2014 review, Tellis 2019) were read only as abstracts or summaries. No Russian-language (RU-market) platform data was searched.

---

## 1. What does research on social sharing say, and how does it apply to short video in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
People share content that stirs **high-arousal emotion** (amusement, awe, excitement, anger, anxiety) and that does a **social job for the sharer**: it makes them look good, bonds them with the person they send it to, or says something about who they are. Content built around **information** tends to suppress sharing, except where the facts lower a real risk for the recipient. A calm, informational explainer starts at a disadvantage for DM sends unless it also carries an emotional or identity charge ("this is so you"). On Instagram that matters a lot, because sends are a top-3 ranking signal and the most important one for reaching non-followers.

### Cited Findings
- **Berger & Milkman (2012, J. Marketing Research)** studied about 7,000 NYT articles and how often they were emailed. High-arousal emotions, positive (awe) or negative (anger, anxiety), make content more viral. Low-arousal emotions (sadness) make it less viral. The arousal effect holds even after controlling for how surprising, interesting or practically useful the content is. Positive content is shared more than negative. **[A]** — [Berger & Milkman PDF (Wharton)](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf); [summary, Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral)
- **Tellis, MacInnis, Tirunillai & Zhang (2019, Journal of Marketing, 83(4))** studied YouTube video ads with 11 emotions and 60+ characteristics. Information-focused content has a significantly negative effect on sharing. The exceptions are risky contexts (new or high-price products), where facts reduce purchase risk. The most-shared videos are emotional, with inspiration, warmth, excitement and amusement. Brand prominence hurts sharing. Emotional content is shared more on general platforms, informational content more on LinkedIn. The factors that boost sharing are "rarely used", and those that hurt it are used extensively. **[A]**, read via summaries only — [AMA "JM Insights" summary](https://www.ama.org/2020/06/11/what-drives-virality-sharing-of-online-digital-content-the-critical-role-of-information-emotion-and-brand-prominence/); [Baylor summary](https://kellercenter.hankamer.baylor.edu/news/story/2020/riding-wave-how-make-your-videos-go-viral)
- **Berger (2014, J. Consumer Psychology) review of word of mouth:** sharing is goal-driven and serves five functions: impression management, emotion regulation, information acquisition, social bonding and persuasion. Impression management works through self-enhancement and identity-signalling. **[A]**, abstract-level only — [EconBiz record](https://econbiz.de/Record/word-of-mouth-and-interpersonal-communication-a-review-and-directions-for-future-research-berger-jonah/10010410173); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/300150218.pdf)
- **Barasch & Berger (2014, JMR):** broadcasting to many people encourages self-presentation, so people avoid sharing content that makes them look bad. Narrowcasting (one-to-one, like a DM) shifts the motive toward being useful to that specific person. **[A]**, secondary mention — [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/300150218.pdf); [AthensCEO summary](https://athensceo.com/news/2014/06/how-audience-size-affects-word-mouth/)
- **Luo & Ng (2025, Social Media + Society)** surveyed 1,000 US adults. Sharing memes (affiliative humour) was associated with greater online "bonding social capital", and through it with well-being. Meme sharing works as a relationship act, not just information transfer. **[A]**, survey and correlational — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/a22b5723b67840ce9ee7b8ccf44cdc32)
- A brand-meme study (Int. J. Advertising, reported June 2026) found that the funnier participants rated the content, the more likely they were to share it. **[A/C]** — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1131037)
- At platform level, Meta has said Reels are reshared about 4.5 billion times a day (CFO Susan Li, Q4 2024 call, via aggregator). Zuckerberg said Reels make up more than half of content shared in Instagram messages. **[B/C]**, secondary transcripts; verify in Meta's own transcripts — [InvestmentWiki Q4 2024](https://investmentwiki.mywikis.eu/wiki/Meta_Platforms:Quarterly_Results/2024_Q4); [MarketBeat transcript](https://www.marketbeat.com/earnings/transcripts/79117)

### Inferences
- A DM send is narrowcasting. The sender asks "will *this person* laugh, feel seen, or find this useful?" Formats that name a shared situation in one glance (POV, "me when…", a meme with relatable text, a skit) do that job instantly: "this is literally you" or "this is us". A 30–45 s explainer forces the sender to watch to the end before they know whether it is worth sending, and the payoff is information. That is exactly the type of content Tellis et al. found suppresses sharing.
- The owner's "cognitive dissonance" hooks create curiosity, which is arousal, but the arousal is spent on a question that the voiceover then *explains*. The emotional peak sits at second 0 and the rest is low-arousal explanation. Berger & Milkman's result implies the share decision follows the emotion, so in an explainer the share impulse fades as the video goes on.
- Humour and "being seen" are the two cheapest high-arousal triggers for an ADHD/procrastination audience. Shame-free self-recognition ("I do this too") plus amusement is a share engine that matches the brand's "no guilt" rule.
- Classic frameworks (Berger's STEPPS from *Contagious*: Social currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical value, Stories) point the same way, but they were **not re-verified this session** (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that measures **DM-send** behaviour on Reels or TikTok specifically, as opposed to email or public shares. The link to DM sends is inferred.
- Full texts of Berger 2014 and Tellis 2019 were not read. Effect sizes are not verified.
- No study found compares "relatable/POV" against "explainer" formats head-to-head on sends.

## 2. What do platforms say, and what does data show on length, voiceover vs audio, text-on-screen, faceless, edutainment and series?

### Takeaway
Instagram has stated in first-party terms that **watch time, likes and sends** are the top three signals, and that **sends per reach matters more for reaching non-followers**. Length data does **not** show 30–45 s as a penalty: a 9.8 M-Reel 2026 dataset shows 30–60 s Reels getting *more* median views than sub-30 s ones (brand accounts). So "too long" is unlikely to be the main cause on its own. Instagram does say it shows fewer Reels with **heavy text overlays** and wants text not to cover most of the screen. Evidence on voiceover vs trending audio, faceless vs face, and series formats is weak (vendor or creator level only).

### Cited Findings
**Platform statements**
- Mosseri, Jan 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends… pay close attention to average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach." Also: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." **[A]** — [Social Media Today, 22 Jan 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)
- Mosseri (2024 Reel) called sends per reach "one of the most important signals we use in ranking". No primary source ranks it above watch time; the claim that it is "the single most important" signal is not supported. **[A/C]** — [Search Engine Journal](https://searchenginejournal.com/instagram-algorithm-shift-why-sends-matter-more-than-ever/521389); [SwipeInsight](https://web.swipeinsight.app/posts/instagram-reveals-sends-per-reach-as-key-ranking-signal-8270)
- Instagram Creators blog (2022, updated Apr 2023) on recommendations to non-followers: "Pull the viewer in within the first 3 seconds, and keep them interested the whole time." Use "high-resolution, 9 x 16 vertical videos with no borders, where text does not cover the majority of the screen." Avoid watermarks, blurry or low-quality video, and reuploaded or unoriginal content. Content must be 3 min or less to be recommended. Only public accounts are recommended. **[A]** — [Instagram for Creators: Recommendations](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators)
- The Instagram Help Center lists Reels it shows less of, including **heavy text overlays**, muted, blurry or low-resolution video, and borders, logos or watermarks. **[A]**, via search snippet — [Instagram Help](https://help.Instagram.com/1525585517644948); background on the 2021 origin: [TubeFilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/02/10/instagram-reels-with-tiktok-watermark-less-discoverable/)
- Mosseri (Oct 2024, via secondary blog): "The first three seconds of a reel are super important"; good hooks "use text and set expectations for what you're about to see". **[C]**, not verified in a primary post — [Inro blog](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-3-second-hook-leads)
- In August 2025 Instagram added **Skip Rate** (share of views skipped within the first 3 s) and a retention graph to Reels Insights, replacing View Rate. **[A/B]**, multiple trade reports — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-retention-chart-skip-rate-new-performance-metrics-reels-9730992); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/)
- Instagram creator event, May 2024 (slide reported via The Information). Things that "can hurt distribution": Reels over 90 s, reposted content, watermarks, engagement bait, poor quality. In Jan 2025 the limit went to 3 min ("90 seconds… just too short for those who want to share longer stories"). **[B]** — [Social Media Today, May 2024](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-reels-hurt-performance); [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-three-minute-reels)
- Originality: Instagram replaces reposts with the original in recommendations and has said smaller original creators were under-recommended relative to aggregators. 2026: accounts dominated by reposts became ineligible for recommendations. **[A/B]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html); [TubeFilter 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/amp/)

**Length data**
- **Socialinsider, 9,771,822 organic brand Reels, Jan–Jun 2026.** Median views: 1–30 s **4,700**; 30–45 s **8,564**; 45–60 s **10,374**; 60–90 s 9,790; 90–120 s 8,000; 120–180 s 9,000; 180 s+ 4,428. Engagement rate (likes+comments/followers): 0–30 s 0.28 %, 30–45 s 0.30 %, 45–60 s 0.35 %, 180 s+ 0.15 %. No completion or watch-time data. Brand accounts only, with an internal inconsistency about account-size scope. **[B]** — [Socialinsider: Ideal Reels length](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
- A Socialinsider 2024 dataset of 100k+ videos, cited secondhand, gives completion by length: 15–30 s 68 %, 30–60 s 51 %, 60–90 s 39 %. Not verified at the original. **[C]** — [AutoClip blog](https://autoclip.dev/blog/best-clip-length-for-each-platform)
- The "7–15 s = 60–80 % retention / highest completion" claim is repeated widely, but OpusClip attributes it only to "multiple studies and creator reports" without naming them. **[C/D]** — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-instagram-reels-length)
- Dash Social (media/entertainment benchmark, H2 2025): on average only about 10 % of users watch a full TikTok. **[B]** — [Dash Social media benchmark](https://www.dashsocial.com/social-media-benchmarks/media-industry)
- A geoscience education TikTok field study (48 videos, 2022 preprint) found videos under 20 s had high retention but low engagement, so overall visibility was lower. Short is not automatically better for educational content. **[A−]**, preprint, single account — [EGUsphere preprint](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2022/egusphere-2022-494/)

**Entertainment vs education**
- Dash Hudson's "Entertainment Score" analysis (7,000+ posts, pre-2025): content scoring 5+ got about 200 % more clicks than content below 5. A Dash Hudson release says entertainment's share of TikTok impact rose from 51 % to 67 % over two years. Release date not confirmed. **[B/C]** — [Dash Hudson glossary](https://dashhudson.com/digital-marketing-dictionary/entertainment-score); [Dash Hudson PDF](https://pages.dashsocial.com/hubfs/guides/how-entertaining-content-leads-to-higher-conversions.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Length is probably not the main culprit.** 30–45 s Reels do at least as well as shorter ones in the largest dataset. That dataset is brands with existing followers, though, and it measures views rather than sends. For a small account, everything hinges on the first test audience of non-followers. There, *sends per reach* and *skip rate* decide expansion, so the question is "does a stranger send it?", not "is it 30 or 15 s?".
- **Text and voiceover load:** the format stacks a large anchor text, word-by-word captions and handwritten labels. Instagram's own guidance says text should not cover most of the screen and that heavy text overlays get shown less. This is a plausible, first-party-backed risk for the current format, worth checking frame by frame.
- **Voiceover explainer = information-first.** Combined with Tellis et al., this predicts lower sharing even when retention is fine. The two numbers Instagram tells creators to watch would split: watch time okay, sends low.
- **Faceless and drawn characters:** no evidence was found that faceless content is penalised. A recurring drawn character can work as a "face" (an emotional proxy). The risk is that a calm explaining voice gives no visible emotional reaction, the "nonverbal" layer that relatable formats get from a face or a skit.

### Gaps
- No credible 2023–2026 data was found comparing **voiceover vs trending audio vs no voice** on Reels reach or sends.
- No credible data was found on **faceless vs face** performance.
- No data was found on **series / recurring character** formats ("part 2", episodic) and their effect on reach or follows. The creator lore (Jenny Hoyos and others) is not quantified.
- No first-party Instagram data was found on the share of entertainment vs educational content among top-sent Reels.
- Completion-by-length numbers for Reels remain unverified at their source.

## 3. How do top educational creators restructure content to be shareable?

### Takeaway
The evidence here is mostly practitioner guidance rather than studies. The consistent pattern, backed by Instagram's own "first 3 seconds" and "set expectations" guidance and by the sharing research, is to front-load the payoff, keep one idea per video, end on a punchline or emotional beat rather than an explanation, and package the identity or relatability angle so the viewer knows within seconds *who to send it to*. Saves-oriented depth goes into carousels, and send-oriented emotion goes into Reels.

### Cited Findings
- Instagram: hook within 3 s and keep interest throughout. Use searchable keywords in captions, since "Search results are matched by text". **[A]** — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators)
- Mosseri (via secondary): good hooks "use text and set expectations for what you're about to see". **[C]** — [Inro blog](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-3-second-hook-leads)
- Tellis et al.: emotion (amusement, inspiration, warmth, excitement) drives sharing, and information suppresses it. Moderate-length videos shared most *for ads* (1.2–1.7 min on YouTube, so not directly transferable to Reels). **[A]** — [AMA summary](https://www.ama.org/2020/06/11/what-drives-virality-sharing-of-online-digital-content-the-critical-role-of-information-emotion-and-brand-prominence/)
- Geoscience TikTok study: very short educational clips retained well but engaged less, so a bit of substance helps engagement. **[A−]** — [EGUsphere](https://egusphere.copernicus.org/preprints/2022/egusphere-2022-494/)
- Mosseri's insight to compare trial Reels against each other on sends, likes and watch time, not raw reach. **[C]**, via Planoly — [Planoly on Trial Reels](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-trial-reels-work-research)

### Inferences
- **A restructure that matches the evidence** for an ADHD and procrastination niche: make the *relatable moment* the content and make the explanation optional.
  - (a) A POV or "me when…" scene of the cat doing the exact avoidance behaviour, readable with sound off in 1–2 s.
  - (b) A punchline or twist at the end (amusement = high arousal), not a lesson.
  - (c) At most one line of insight, framed as "it's not laziness" (identity relief, shame-free). It gives the sender a reason to forward: "this is why you do it."
- Keep the explainer depth for carousels or long-form, where saves and watch time matter, and use Reels as the send-bait top of the funnel.
- **A series with a recurring character** (the cat as a consistent "ADHD roommate") is a reasonable bet, because it adds recognisability and gives viewers a reason to follow. This is an inference with **no quantified evidence** found.

### Gaps
- No rigorous case-study data was found (e.g., an educational creator's before/after metrics when switching to relatable formats). Most material is unsourced creator advice and was excluded.
- No data was found on carousel save rates vs Reel save rates for educational content in 2024–2026.

## 4. Why do small accounts' Reels stall, and do off-platform CTAs (link in bio, Telegram) reduce reach?

### Takeaway
On official evidence, **saying "link in bio" does not reduce reach**: Mosseri has said so in 2020 and again in July 2025. No evidence was found that mentioning **Telegram** is downranked, and no published guideline mentions off-platform links. What *is* first-party is that engagement bait, watermarks, low quality, unoriginal content, heavy text overlays and Reels over 3 min are shown less to non-followers. For a 100–200-view account, the likelier causes are early-audience signals (skip rate in the first 3 s, sends per reach, watch time) and an information-first format, not a CTA penalty. A CTA that asks the viewer to leave the platform still gives them no reason to *send* the Reel, and engagement-bait-style comment prompts can hurt.

### Cited Findings
- Mosseri, Threads, reported 23 Jul 2025: "if you say 'link in bio' it's going to decrease your reach. That is not true… it will not affect your reach one way or another." He said it can be "super helpful" and that he uses it himself. **[A]** — [Social Media Today, Jul 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-doesnt-penalize-posts-that-include-link-in-bio/753899/); 2020 version: [PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2020/08/20/lightroom-app-update-wipes-users-photos-and-presets-adobe-says-they-are-not-recoverable/link)
- Anecdotal reports of lower reach on posts that mention external links persist, and eMarketer calls off-platform links an ongoing "tension" for creators. **[C/D]** — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/off-platform-links-remain-tension-instagram-creators); a review of the measurements found them inconsistent and noted Instagram's ranking explainer does not mention links — [Blck Alpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-algorithms-distribution/what-is-a-shadowban-myths-and-evidence)
- **No test was found of Instagram captions mentioning Telegram** or another competitor platform. **[—]** — search returned only unrelated items, e.g. [Agorapulse caption test](https://www.agorapulse.com/social-media-lab/instagram-caption/) (blank captions: −22 % reach, single account, **[C]**)
- **Engagement bait** is listed among factors that "can hurt distribution" (May 2024 creator-event slide). Third-party guides say Recommendation Guidelines cover clickbait and engagement bait; the exact first-party wording was not found. **[B/C]** — [Social Media Today, May 2024](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-longer-reels-hurt-performance); [Inro guide](https://www.inro.social/blog/dos-and-donts-to-reach-new-audiences-on-instagram-organic-reach-guide)
- Recommendation eligibility (the non-follower surface) is where Instagram limits reach. Account Status shows whether an account is eligible for recommendations, and repeated guideline breaches can make a whole account ineligible for a period. **[A]** — [Instagram for Creators](https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-recommendations-eligibility-tips-creators); [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/07/instagrams-new-transparency-tools-will-tell-you-if-your-content-is-ineligible-to-be-recommended/)
- **Trial Reels** (shown only to non-followers) are available to public accounts with **≥ 1,000 followers**. Mosseri said Instagram was "considering" smaller accounts. Instagram claimed that of creators who posted Reels more often, 80 % saw more non-follower reach. Small accounts on Reddit report trials stuck at a few hundred views. **[B/C]** — [Social Media Today, Jun 2025](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-expands-access-trial-reels/752895/); [Planoly](https://www.planoly.com/blog/do-trial-reels-work-research)
- Skip Rate (swipes in the first 3 s) is now a first-party metric for diagnosing a weak opening. **[A/B]** — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/instagram-retention-chart-skip-rate-new-performance-metrics-reels-9730992)
- Instagram's originality push says it wants to give smaller original creators a more equal chance with non-followers. Eligible content is first shown to a small audience predicted to enjoy it. **[A/B]** — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)

### Inferences
- **100–200 views is the size of the initial test pool.** The Reel is not expanding past the first non-follower sample, which by Mosseri's own metrics means skip rate, watch time and/or sends per reach in that sample are below what the system expects for similar content.
- **Likely causes, ranked by evidence fit:**
  1. **Low sends per reach.** The format is information-first, and the sharing research predicts low forwarding.
  2. **First-frame and skip rate.** A drawn paper scene with a text anchor may not be instantly decodable as "about me", and Instagram flags heavy text overlays.
  3. **The "explain" payoff** means no high-arousal peak at the end that would trigger a send.
  4. **CTA.** Not a reach penalty by official accounts, but it spends the final seconds on leaving rather than on a send-worthy punchline. If it uses "comment WORD" mechanics, it risks the engagement-bait flag.
  5. **Length 30–45 s** is the weakest-supported cause in the data.
- **Diagnose before rebuilding.** Read the Insights numbers Mosseri recommends for each Reel: Skip Rate, the retention graph, sends per reach, likes per reach and the non-follower share. Check Account Status for recommendation eligibility. Compare a relatable/POV variant against an explainer on sends per reach. This turns "format change needed?" into a measurable A/B test.
- The Telegram-mention penalty hypothesis has **no evidence either way**. A matched-pair test (same style, with and without the mention) is the only way to settle it for this account.

### Gaps
- No first-party statement was found about mentions of **competitor messaging apps** (Telegram, WhatsApp) in captions or voice.
- The exact text of Instagram's Recommendation Guidelines on engagement bait and clickbait was not retrieved. The Help page fetch returned empty.
- No data was found on how many views a typical new Reel from a sub-1k account gets in its initial test, so there is no baseline for whether 100–200 is normal or low.
- Nothing was found on Russian-speaking-audience behaviour specifically.
